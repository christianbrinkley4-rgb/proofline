# Competitive brief

Researched September 28, 2026 (updates the September 24 review). Prices and user counts come from vendor pages and third-party reviews published this month. Vendor outcome claims ("doubles interview rates") are marketing, not independent results. Where two sources disagree on a price, the higher current list price is shown.

## Executive summary

The market is splitting in two. One side races for **volume**: auto-apply agents (Jobright), free form autofill (Simplify), LinkedIn's Apply Assistant, and AI writers that produce a full resume from a job title (Kickresume). The other side, **hiring teams, is drowning in that volume**: LinkedIn reports about 11,000 applications a minute, up 45% in a year, and in Robert Half's 2026 survey 65% of hiring managers say skills are harder to verify on AI-optimized resumes. NACE now writes about "the ghostwritten candidate."

**Biggest opportunity:** nobody credibly owns *truth*. Every competitor promises more applications or higher match scores; none can show a recruiter, or the applicant, that each line on the page is real. Proofline already enforces that in code (confirmed facts, a claim linter, export gates). It is the product's moat, and the landing page barely says it.

**Biggest threat:** Proofline is behind on everything around the resume: finding jobs, filling forms, editing freely, interview practice, LinkedIn, and distribution. Several of these are built but hidden in the private beta. Handshake (campus distribution, AI Skills Studio launched September 22, 2026) and LinkedIn (Premium job match, profile rewrites, Apply Assistant) can add "good enough" resume tailoring faster than we can add their networks.

## Competitor profiles

### Teal
- **Position:** full job-search platform (builder, tracker, Chrome extension, AI writing).
- **Price:** free plan with unlimited resumes, unlimited tracking, 10 templates, limited AI credits. Teal+ is $13/week, $29/month, or $79/quarter; no annual plan.
- **Strengths:** mature editor (drag-and-drop sections, many versions), live job-description keyword checks, LinkedIn import, and a large content library that ranks for resume-advice searches.
- **Weaknesses:** weekly billing targets people under pressure; the AI writes what you ask, true or not; match scores reward keyword stuffing.

### Rezi, Kickresume, Enhancv, Resume.io
- **Position:** "AI resume builder." Rezi sells ATS and keyword targeting; Kickresume sells volume (a full draft from a job title, 20,000+ prewritten phrases, mobile apps, interview prep); Enhancv sells design.
- **Student offer:** Kickresume gives students six months of Premium free.
- **Weaknesses:** prewritten phrases and title-only drafts are exactly the generic AI text recruiters say they spot in seconds. The cheap builders (Resume.io and similar) draw a steady stream of complaints about low-cost trials that renew as subscriptions and cancellations that don't stop charges.

### Jobscan
- **Position:** the resume-versus-posting scanner; says its score is an estimate, not an ATS score.
- **Price:** 5 free scans a month; Premium $49.95/month, $89.95/quarter, or about $300/year. Refunds only within 2 days, only if unused, minus a 3.5% fee.
- **Strengths:** detailed keyword, skills, formatting, and searchability report; LinkedIn profile optimization; cover letters; tracker.
- **Weaknesses:** optimizes the document for a score, not the person's evidence; says nothing about whether a claim is true.

### Simplify
- **Position:** free autofill and tracking. Copilot fills applications on 100+ portals including Workday, Greenhouse, iCIMS, Taleo, Lever, and SmartRecruiters; the person reviews and submits.
- **Price:** autofill and tracking free; Simplify+ (AI resumes, cover letters) about $40/month or $90/quarter, with weekly plans.
- **Strengths:** the free extension is the acquisition channel; saved answers remove the worst part of applying.
- **Weaknesses:** tailoring is paywalled and ungrounded.

### Huntr
- **Position:** job tracker and CRM with a one-click clipper, AI tailoring, application packets, autofill, and contacts.
- **Price:** free for 100 tracked jobs; Pro $40/month, $90/quarter, $160 per six months.
- **Weaknesses:** $40/month for a tracker draws public criticism; AI output is not checked against the person's history.

### Jobright
- **Position:** "AI job search copilot." Personalized matches from resume, seniority, goals, location, and pay; an agent that finds, customizes, and submits applications.
- **Scale claims:** 520,000+ users, growing 30x year over year, "doubling interview rates" (vendor claims).
- **Price:** $14.99/week, $29.99/month, $69.99/quarter.
- **Weaknesses:** independent reviews call the auto-apply agent beta-grade assisted autofill rather than the autonomy the marketing implies; auto-apply only multiplies whatever resume it is given, and it feeds the flood recruiters now filter against.

### LinkedIn Premium Career
- **Position:** the network plus AI: job-fit assessments, AI profile rewrites, message drafting, Premium Apply Assistant (prefill plus cover letters), applicant insights, salary data, 5 InMails a month.
- **Price:** $29.99/month or $239.88/year.
- **Threat level:** high. It owns the profile recruiters actually search, and "good enough" tailoring inside LinkedIn removes the reason to open another tool.

### Handshake
- **Position:** "the career network for the AI economy," distributed through career centers.
- **Recent moves:** acquired Uplimit (June 2026) and launched AI Skills Studio on September 22, 2026: free 10 to 45 minute project "missions" co-designed with OpenAI, Salesforce, and Notion that become hiring evidence. Also AI job search, ranked recommendations, and a job-specific assistant that suggests profile and resume improvements.
- **Threat level:** highest for Proofline's student audience. Handshake is turning "build a real project" into profile evidence, which is close to Proofline's thesis, and it already has the campuses.

### Final Round AI
- **Position:** mock interviews, a question bank, and a live copilot that listens in real interviews and suggests answers.
- **Price:** $150/month, or $25/month billed yearly ($300 up front); free plan includes unlimited mock interviews but no live copilot. 72-hour refund on first quarterly or yearly purchases only.
- **Weaknesses:** the live copilot is covert assistance in a real interview; employers increasingly screen for it.

### ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini
The real default for students: free and fast, and they will invent a metric if asked for "a stronger bullet." Proofline's bring-your-own-AI connector (hidden in the beta) turns this competitor into a channel.

## Where Proofline is ahead

- **Every line is traceable.** Claims trace to confirmed facts; the linter blocks unconfirmed claims, em dashes, and overflow; a model review is fingerprinted to the exact resume. No competitor does this.
- **Numbers you can defend.** Model-written numbers must match cited facts. Competitors' AI will invent "increased engagement 40%."
- **Knockouts before the score.** Graduation window, authorization, location, and start date are shown separately instead of being blended into a flattering number.
- **Asks instead of guessing.** Gaps become questions; answers become facts and new bullets.
- **Honest scoring language.** Fit is evidence alignment, not a hiring probability. Jobscan says the same about its score; the others imply more.
- **Free, with your own AI.** The core runs on rules at $0, and a student can connect their own model over MCP.

## Opportunities

1. **Own "true" as a category.** Reframe the category from "AI resume builder" to "the resume you can defend in the interview." Villain: AI slop and mass-apply spam. Hero: the student with real, specific evidence.
2. **Recruiter-side proof.** An optional, consented link that shows which facts back each line would give recruiters a reason to trust a Proofline resume. Nobody offers this.
3. **Honest pricing as positioning.** Competitors cluster at $29 to $50 a month with weekly plans and strict refunds, and billing complaints dominate review sites. "Free for students; no weekly plans; cancel in one click" is a message nobody else can claim.
4. **Career centers want authenticity.** NACE is publishing about ghostwritten candidates. Career centers are a distribution channel Handshake occupies but has not aimed at truthfulness.
5. **Assisted, not automatic, applying.** Simplify proved people want autofill; Jobright shows the backlash risk of auto-submit. A review-first extension that fills only confirmed answers sits between them.

## Threats

- Handshake adds resume tailoring on top of Skills Studio evidence, inside the product students already use.
- LinkedIn's Apply Assistant and profile rewrites make "good enough" tailoring free inside the application flow.
- A general assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) ships a job-search mode with memory; students never leave it.
- ATS vendors start penalizing volume applicants, which hurts everyone, including honest ones, unless recruiters can tell the difference.
